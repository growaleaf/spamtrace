# Spamtrace

Paste a spam text message. Find out how they got your number — and exactly how to shut it off.

Runs **entirely in the browser**. The message you paste is never transmitted anywhere: no server,
no analytics, no cookies. A privacy tool that uploads your data would be a contradiction.

## What it does

1. **Classifies the message** into one of 17 spam archetypes by textual fingerprint.
2. **Triages** — the single most important output. For a scam it says *do not reply, not even STOP*
   (a reply proves the number is live and increases volume). For real marketing it says *reply STOP,
   not their custom keyword*, and explains why.
3. **Traces the supply chain** stage by stage, naming the real vendors that operate at each step.
4. **Explains a wrong name** — why a recycled phone number carries a stranger's identity.
5. **Orders the removal actions upstream-first**, because cleaning a people-search site before the
   upstream aggregator just gets you re-imported on the next refresh.
6. **Writes the regulatory complaint for you**, ready to paste into the FCC/FTC/CFPB/IC3 form.

## Data

Broker names, opt-out URLs and 2024 deletion-request statistics come from the
[California Data Broker Registry](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_broker_registry/) — 581 registered brokers
who must declare this information under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.99.82, with administrative penalties for
false filings. These are the companies' *own* declarations, not a third-party guess.

Legal text is quoted from [47 CFR § 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)
as fetched from eCFR.

Every outbound link was fetched and confirmed working on 2026-07-29. Where no working opt-out URL
could be verified, the site omits it rather than shipping a guess.
